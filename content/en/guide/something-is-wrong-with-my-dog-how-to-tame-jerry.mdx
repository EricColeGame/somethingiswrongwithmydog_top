Learning **how to tame Jerry** is the difference between stumbling through your first night alone and having a companion who follows you across the map, warns you about the woods, and rewards you with a stack of badges.

The good news is that the process is short. The short answer to **how to tame Jerry** is simple: hand him exactly three bones at the treeline before you push deeper into the forest. ## How to Tame Jerry: Step-by-Step

The taming sequence happens in the first few minutes of a run, which means you can't afford to wander off toward the cabin before you've handled it. *Something is Wrong With My Dog* — developed by the group • DELTA • and inspired by LookOut2DD's horror series of the same name — puts Jerry directly in your path, so the encounter is hard to miss.

### Step 1: Spot Jerry at the Treeline

Look toward the edge of the woods as you begin searching for Uncle Ray. The wolf is deliberately placed to catch your eye: thin frame, grey coat, red collar. Spotting him triggers the "Welcome to the Woods" badge, which is your confirmation that you've found the right character and not a background prop.

### Step 2: Make Sure You're Carrying Three Bones

Check your inventory before you approach. If you only have one or two bones, Jerry will not bond with you — the requirement is a full three. Bones come from story exploration, mining, and world spawns, so most players already have a few by the time they reach the treeline. If you don't, the next section covers the fastest ways to stock up.

### Step 3: Equip the Bones and Interact

Press **E** to open your inventory, select your bones, and walk up to Jerry. Then use **Right Click** — the default Place / Use key — to offer a bone. Repeat until all three are consumed.

### Step 4: Confirm the Bond

Once the third bone is gone, Jerry becomes yours. He starts trailing behind you, follows you back toward camp, and the "Good Boy" badge fires automatically. If the badge doesn't appear, you haven't actually fed three bones — recount them in your inventory.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Spot Jerry at the treeline | Look toward the forest edge | Welcome to the Woods |
| 2 | Gather three bones | Exploration, mining, spawns | — |
| 3 | Equip bones from inventory | E | — |
| 4 | Feed Jerry three times | Right Click | Good Boy |
| 5 | Survive until morning | Return to the cabin | Morning |

### Controls You'll Actually Use

Most of the taming friction comes from players pressing the wrong button. Left Click is for attacking and breaking blocks, which is exactly what you don't want to do in front of a wolf you're trying to befriend.

## Bone Farming: How to Get Three Bones Fast

Three bones sounds trivial until you realize you spent your first two on nothing important. Here's where they come from and how to plan around it.

| Bone Source | How It Works | Best For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story exploration | Bones are scattered along the early path into the woods | Players who follow the story beats |
| Mining | Breaking blocks and digging into rock formations yields bones | Players who like to loot before progressing |
| World spawns | Bones appear as pickups around the map | Topping off a partial stack |
| 2x Bones game pass | Multiplies bone gains | Repeat runs and Survival Mode grinding |

A practical tip from player experience: don't spend bones on anything else until Jerry is tamed. Because the requirement is a hard three, a single impulsive use can send you back into the woods to farm again while the night timer keeps ticking.

Community reports also suggest grabbing a fourth bone if you can. Not for taming — Jerry only accepts three — but so you have a spare for later healing interactions.

## Life With Jerry After Taming

Taming Jerry is the start of the relationship, not the end of it. Once he's bonded, he follows you across the map while you gather materials, explore caves, and track down Bob's cabin. He effectively becomes a second set of eyes in a game built around things going wrong at night.

| Behavior | What Happens |
| --- | --- |
| Following | Jerry trails behind you anywhere you travel on the map |
| Taking damage | He can be hurt during encounters and needs food to recover |
| Healing | Feeding him after damage unlocks the Treat Time badge |
| Morning routine | Surviving to daybreak triggers strange events near your bed |
| Renaming | An optional game pass lets you change his name, which updates in story dialogue |

The morning behavior is the part that gives the game its title. According to the game's own description, Jerry leaves something dead beside your bed each morning. Surviving until first light and seeing that scene through awards the Morning badge, along with Jerry's collar. It's a strong hint that your new best friend is not entirely a good boy — which is exactly the tone *Something is Wrong With My Dog* is going for.

There's also an optional "Name Jerry" game pass listed at 79 Robux that lets you assign a custom name, and that name carries into story run dialogue. Purely cosmetic, but a nice touch if you've gotten attached.

## Common Taming Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Most failed taming attempts come down to one of five issues. Here's the troubleshooting table.

| Problem | Likely Cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jerry won't accept food | You're using Left Click instead of Right Click | Switch to the Place / Use key |
| Bond never completes | You fed fewer than three bones | Recount your inventory and farm more |
| Can't find Jerry at all | You moved past the treeline too quickly | Restart the run and scan the forest edge on spawn |
| Jerry dies early | You pushed into encounters without healing him | Keep spare food and feed him after fights |
| No Morning badge | You didn't survive to daybreak at the cabin | Prioritize shelter over loot on night one |

One more note that surprises people: the game has both a Story mode and a dedicated Survival Mode that share the same world map but skip the scripted checkpoints. Jerry still shows up in Survival Mode, but the taming flow is looser — you're surviving nights with him roaming the environment rather than following a set narrative. If you're purely testing **how to tame Jerry**, Story mode is the cleaner classroom.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many bones do you need to tame Jerry?

Exactly three. The Good Boy badge requirement confirms that offering three bones is what binds Jerry to your character and makes him follow you home. Extra bones don't speed anything up, but keeping one spare for healing is smart.

### Which button do you press to feed Jerry?

Right Click is the default Place / Use interaction, and it's what feeds Jerry. Left Click attacks and breaks blocks, so avoid it near him. Open your inventory first with E to select the bones.

### Can you tame Jerry outside of Story mode?

Yes. Survival Mode shares the same map without scripted story checkpoints, and Jerry is still present in the world. You'll need to survive nights while he roams the environment rather than following a fixed narrative path.

### What happens after you tame Jerry in Something is Wrong With My Dog?

He follows you across the map, can be healed with food, and every morning after you survive the night he leaves something dead beside your bed. That sequence leads to the Morning badge and Jerry's collar — and a pretty clear warning about what you've brought home.

Knowing **how to tame Jerry** early turns a hostile forest into a manageable one. Grab three bones, find the red collar at the treeline, and use Right Click — then pay attention to what shows up next to your bed in the morning.

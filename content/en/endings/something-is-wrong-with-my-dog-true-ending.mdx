## A Practical Route to the True Ending

Here is the route most players describe when they finally land the **Something Is Wrong With My Dog true ending**. It is not a speedrun — it is a thoroughness plan.

### 1. Slow down the opening twenty minutes

The first area is where most players lose the true ending without realizing it. Treat the opening as a checklist zone: every room, every interactable, every line of text.

### 2. Exhaust dialogue instead of sampling it

Click or press through until the text starts repeating. Games often distinguish between "you heard it" and "you finished it," and only the second one sets the flag.

### 3. Revisit rooms after each tonal shift

The environment usually changes after major beats. A door that was locked, a hallway that was dark, an object that was not there before — those changes are content, not decoration.

### 4. Keep one clean save at the point of no return

This is the single highest-value habit in any ending hunt. One manual save before the finale turns a four-hour replay into a ten-minute experiment.

### 5. Verify before you commit

Cross-check your progress against the achievement list, then commit to the final sequence. If the finale looks identical to your first run, you missed a condition — reload rather than finishing.

| Phase | What you are doing | Signal you are on track |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Full sweep of the first area | You found content you missed on run one |
| Middle | Dialogue and environment completion | New lines or scenes appear after revisits |
| Late | Confirming optional flags | Achievement or in-game indicator progress |
| Finale | Committing from a clean save | The final scene plays differently than before |

## Mistakes That Quietly Lock You Out

These are the recurring culprits in community threads about the **Something Is Wrong With My Dog true ending**. None of them are dramatic; all of them are easy to do.

- **Rushing the opening.** The most common cause of a default ending, by a wide margin.
- **Clicking once per dialogue box.** Treating dialogue as a formality instead of a requirement.
- **Trusting autosave.** Autosave overwrites the exact state you may want to return to.
- **Assuming optional means unimportant.** In ending-driven games, optional content *is* the ending.
- **Playing on an outdated build.** Conditions have been adjusted in updates; an old guide and a new build can disagree.
- **Finishing "to see what happens."** If the finale looks wrong, reload instead of pushing through.

| Mistake | Likely outcome | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping the first area | Default ending | Full sweep on a fresh save |
| Partial dialogue | Missing flag, partial ending | Re-talk until lines repeat |
| No manual save | Full replay to retry | Named save before the finale |
| Outdated build | Mismatched conditions | Check the current patch |
